# System-level intelligence — the corpus as the unit of analysis (Pillar F, the capstone) (2026-08-09)

The honest gap this closes: everything planned and built so far is PAIRWISE and ITEM-CENTRIC — "for an
item, find its connections," one brief at a time, page by page. Nothing analyses the WHOLE corpus. This is
the roadmap for the layer above: the system that treats the connection graph itself as the substrate and
synthesises across everything — themes, convergences, pivots, trajectories. It is the apex of the master
plan (Pillar F), and it is where the product's real value lives: an investor does not act on one regulation;
they act on a THEME converging across many instruments and surfaces with a trajectory and a window. Item
connection is the plumbing; corpus synthesis is the product.

## Why this is the capstone, not an add-on

- **"Get ahead of others" at scale** is seeing the CONVERGENCE forming, not the single pair. A competitor can, with effort, notice that reg X relates to market signal Y. What they cannot reproduce is: "a Scope-3 supplier-data-mandate wave is forming across 14 instruments in EU/IMO/US, spanning Regulations + Research + Market, accelerating toward 2027 — here is the trajectory and the participation windows." That is corpus-level, provenance-grounded, and the moat.
- **"Make money long-term through investments"** is thesis-level. A thesis is a cluster + a trajectory + a timing window, not a brief. Until the system produces that object, the analyst has to assemble it by hand from many pages — exactly the "page by page" limit.
- It **sits on Pillar A**: the connection graph is the substrate. Once edges exist (A2 backfill), most of this layer is $0 ALGORITHMIC on the graph — clustering, centrality, convergence, trajectory need no model. Only the synthesis PROSE is metered, and it is the last, gated step.

## Dependency: needs the populated graph

Pillar A (discover + backfill) must run first — clusters cannot form on 61 edges. Once the backfill
populates provenance-grounded edges, this layer becomes buildable. F1-F4 are graph algorithms over those
edges ($0); F5 is the metered synthesis; F6 is the standing maintenance loop.

## The layer (built on the connection graph)

**F1 — Theme detection (clustering).** Run community detection over the connection graph → clusters of
densely-connected items = THEMES. Each cluster is a first-class system object with an id, its member items,
its dominant shared signals (the scenario tags / instruments that bind it), and the surfaces it spans.
Graph-algorithmic, $0. Output: the corpus's actual themes, discovered from the data, not a hand-taxonomy.

**F2 — Pivot / hub detection (centrality).** Items with high connection centrality = pivotal instruments
(a foundational directive many others amend/implement/depend on; a hub market signal). These are the
"watch these — they move everything" anchors. Graph centrality, $0.

**F3 — Cross-surface convergence detection.** A cluster that spans MULTIPLE surfaces (Regulations +
Research + Market + Operations all connected on one theme) is a CONVERGENCE — the strongest "this is
happening, get positioned" signal, and the differentiator at system scale. Score each cluster by
surface-span × density × recency. The top convergences are the corpus's headline intelligence. $0.

**F4 — Trajectory / horizon analysis.** Order a cluster's items by date + effective-date + forward events
(the forward-participation pathway from B1) → the THEME'S TRAJECTORY: where it is heading, whether it is
accelerating, the next windows. This is the investment-timing layer. Mostly $0 (temporal ordering over the
cluster + the already-extracted forward events).

**F5 — System-level synthesis briefs (a new artifact type).** For a convergence/theme, a THEME BRIEF:
analysis ABOUT the cluster, synthesised across its member items, grounded in the connection graph and the
members' own verified claims. "The Scope-3 supplier-data theme: N instruments across EU/IMO/US, converging
on X, trajectory to 2027, the pivots, the windows, what it changes for a freight operator." This is the
portfolio-level object an investor reads to form a thesis. It is the metered step — gated, pilot-first,
$0-in-session-authorable via the same engine — and grounded: no theme asserted that the graph doesn't show,
no claim not traceable to a member item's verified provenance. It composes with read-time anchoring (the
theme lensed to the viewer's profile) and lives on a system-level surface (a "Themes / Convergences" view)
above the five page surfaces.

**F6 — The management loop (what maintains it).** The system-level model is not static: as the scan
collects and the interpret tools populate edges, clusters grow/split/merge, new convergences form,
trajectories update. A periodic pass re-runs F1-F4 over the current graph and flags what CHANGED — a new
convergence forming, a theme accelerating, a pivot emerging. THAT delta is the highest-value alert: not "a
regulation changed" but "a THEME crossed a threshold." This is the "manage all of this analysis on a larger
level" layer — the corpus watching itself.

## Governance (same guarantees, at system scale)

- Grounded in the graph: a theme is a real cluster of real edges, each edge with its basis; a convergence is
  a real multi-surface span. No invented themes, no ungrounded synthesis — the moat extends to the apex.
- Algorithmic layers (F1-F4) are $0 and run freely; the synthesis prose (F5) is metered, behind the budget
  kill-switch, pilot-measured before any corpus-wide run.
- Every system-level claim traces to member items' verified provenance — the theme brief cites its cluster.

## Sequencing into the master plan

Pillar F is the capstone, after A (graph) and composing with B (grounded generation), C (data), D (surface).
Order: A2 backfill populates the graph → F1-F4 (the $0 algorithmic corpus view — themes, pivots,
convergences, trajectories) → a system-level surface (D-class UI) to see them → F5 theme briefs (the metered
apex, pilot-first) → F6 the maintenance loop. F1-F3 are the fastest high-value win after the backfill: the
corpus's real themes and convergences, discovered and visible, for zero marginal cost.
